# Which Voice Are You Being True To?

*On the slogan we use to excuse almost anything*

"Just be yourself."

"I'm only being authentic."

"I'm being true to myself."

These sentences are everywhere, and they sound like freedom. Nobody wants to argue with them. But they rest on an assumption almost nobody examines: that we already know who "myself" is.

Most of us don't. We take the personality we were shaped into for the self, and we claim everything that moves through us as ours. A thought arrives, a craving, a sudden contempt, an urge to withdraw or to attack, and it is stamped "mine" before we have looked at it. And if it is mine, then acting on it is authenticity.

Run that logic for a minute and see where it goes. A trauma response becomes authenticity. A narcissistic outburst becomes authenticity. Projecting my shadow onto you becomes me being honest. A belief absorbed from my family, my culture or my religion before I could question it becomes my truth. Even a desire that was never mine at all becomes who I really am.

## Whose voice is it

Marion Woodman, the Jungian analyst, said there is no sense talking about being true to yourself

> until you are sure what voice you are being true to.

*Marion Woodman*

She also said it takes hard work to tell the voices of the unconscious apart. That sentence carries the whole problem. There are many voices inside us, and they do not introduce themselves. The wounded child speaks in the first person. So does the shame, the social program, the part that learned to survive by performing.

Robert A. Johnson, who trained in the Jungian tradition and also spent time at the Sri Aurobindo Ashram, treated the word differentiation as something close to sacred. For him it was not intellectual discernment. It pointed somewhere much deeper, and it sits at the foundation of what "know thyself" actually asks of us.

## It does not fit in a list

This is why it frustrates me when inner work gets packaged into a short post with ten points. There is no list that tells you, this is my shadow, this is an inner child part, this is something else entirely. Frameworks can orient us. They cannot discern for us.

Differentiation grows slowly, through years of the unglamorous things. Self-observation. Radical honesty. Shadow work and inner child work. Paying attention to the body. Meditation. Psychological maturity. And the repeated, uncomfortable return to whatever we would rather not see in ourselves.

Over time something changes. We get familiar with the texture of our own psyche, the way you learn the sound of a voice you hear every day. We begin to feel, in the body and not in the head, the difference between an abandonment wound and a shame complex, between a trauma response and a belief we inherited, between a projection and a genuine perception. And behind all of it, quietly, something that was never any of those things.

None of this means becoming suspicious of every thought. It means loosening the reflex that says "I" and "mine" to everything that passes through.

## Not everything that enters is ours

From an occult perspective, which is the one I work from, there is another layer to this. Some of what moves through a person does not originate in them. If every thought and impulse is claimed as mine, nothing foreign can ever be recognised as foreign, and manipulation from the astral realms becomes very easy. It tends to enter exactly where we are already wounded or already conditioned, through a point that is real and already open.

It also means learning to tell inspiration that comes from the Divine from influences in the lower astral that imitate it well. The imitation can be convincing. That is the point of it.

Telling what to integrate from what to refuse is a threshold, and it is one of the places where the sincere seeker is separated from the one who only likes the language.

## The danger of hurry

None of this can be rushed, and the impatient ego hates that. Impatience is not a neutral trait on this path. It draws in whatever offers a shortcut.

This is also why pop-spiritual teachings, and especially a lot of channeled material, are so appealing right now. They are fast, they flatter, and they fit a short attention span. They let us feel advanced without the confrontation that would make us so. Instead of supporting real development they feed spiritual bypassing, and the seeker ends up overestimating their own level of being and wandering a maze they believe is a path.

I don't think "be yourself" is a wrong sentence. I think it is an unfinished one. It needs a question in front of it: which self? I would rather keep asking that for the rest of my life than answer it too quickly.
